Data-center growth is concentrating water demand in stressed basins, yet facility-level evidence remains scarce. We reconstruct the Scope 1/2/3 water footprint of 243 data-center buildings in Prince William County, Virginia, using public records only. Fifty-four buildings currently operate, with estimated water use of 10.5 MGD; full buildout could reach 49.6 MGD. We quantify uncertainty in inferred power demand and test our estimates against utility-reported 2023 data compiled by Virginia's legislative audit commission. The distributions are statistically indistinguishable (KS p = 0.09), although the benchmark includes Prince William County and is therefore not fully independent.

Indirect water accounts for 87% of the estimated footprint. We find that two accounting approaches point to different watersheds. Allocating grid water use across electricity consumers assigns more than 40% of electricity-related water to Lake Anna, 80 km outside the host basin. Asking instead what water use an added load causes assigns none to Lake Anna because nuclear does not appear among reported marginal resources. Neither approach is inherently incorrect, but basin plans based on one do not transfer to the other. We also find that floor-area-inferred power carries approximately +/-60% uncertainty, calibrated by leave-one-out testing (90% coverage 0.86), while operator-disclosed load is unavailable for all 243 buildings. Finally, temporal resolution changes the apparent level of stress. The draw in the basin containing most buildings is equivalent to 4% of mean annual flow but 23-34% of July flow. Notably, the regional water authority's own assessment of this basin covers on-site use only. These results show that data-center water burdens cannot be understood from facility-level consumption alone and that accounting choices, data transparency, and seasonal resolution materially affect where water stress appears.
