Data-center growth is concentrating water demand in stressed basins, yet facility-level consumption is rarely measured or disclosed. We ask how much of the apparent burden is set by measurement choices rather than by measurement error.

We reconstruct the water footprint of 243 data-center buildings in Prince William County, Virginia - 54 operating today, the rest permitted or under construction - from public records alone: air permits, utility filings, transmission plans, and county GIS. Adapting the carbon-accounting scope framework to water, estimated use is 10 million gallons per day (MGD) now, rising to roughly 50 MGD at full buildout. Critically, 87% is consumed not at the buildings but at the power plants supplying them; the remainder splits between on-site cooling and supply chain.

Because that majority is inferred rather than metered, three methodological choices each change the apparent answer. First, spatial attribution: allocating grid water across all electricity users assigns more than 40% of it to Lake Anna, 80 km outside the host basin, whereas asking which plants respond to an added load assigns none, since nuclear is not among reported marginal resources. Second, evidence: floor-area-inferred power carries roughly +/-60% uncertainty (leave-one-out calibrated, 90% coverage 0.86), and no operator publishes the load that would resolve it. Third, temporal resolution: the draw in the basin holding most buildings equals 4% of mean annual flow but 23-34% of July flow.

None of these choices is incorrect, but they imply different watersheds, different confidence, and different seasons of concern - a basin plan built on one does not transfer to another. Validation against utility-reported 2023 data compiled by Virginia's legislative audit commission is consistent at the distribution level (KS p = 0.09, n = 54), though that benchmark covers the study county and is not fully independent. The regional water authority's own assessment of this basin addresses on-site use only - under one-twentieth of the footprint we estimate.
