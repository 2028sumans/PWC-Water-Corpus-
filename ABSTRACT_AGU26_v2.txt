Data-center growth is increasing water demand in already stressed basins, yet facility-level evidence remains limited because operators rarely disclose water use or electricity demand. We reconstruct the water footprint of 54 operating data-center buildings in Prince William County, Virginia, from public records, adapting a carbon-accounting scope framework to distinguish on-site cooling (Scope 1), electricity generation (Scope 2), and supply-chain water use (Scope 3). Scope 2 accounts for 88% of the combined footprint, so most associated water is consumed at power plants rather than at the facilities. Because electricity drawn from a shared grid cannot be traced to a particular generator, assigning that consumption to a consumer requires an accounting convention, and the choice is consequential. Under average accounting, which distributes water used by all generation across all consumers, more than 40% of electricity-related water use is assigned to Lake Anna, 80 km away in the York basin rather than the host Potomac. Under short-run marginal accounting, which identifies the generators responding to incremental demand using 2022 PJM marginal fuel shares, under 2% is attributed there. These approaches are not equivalent: average accounting apportions a shared pool, whereas marginal accounting estimates the additional generation a new load actually causes. Neither changes where water is physically lost. They change which basin is charged with it, and therefore which regulators and communities enter the scope of assessment, and the conventions in institutional use are the apportioning ones. Power inferred from floor area carries roughly +-60% uncertainty and no operator discloses the load data needed to resolve it; because every scope scales with the same power estimate, this cancels in the shares reported here but not in absolute volumes. The regional water authority's own assessment of this basin addresses Scope 1 only, under 3% of our estimate, so the largest component of the footprint is currently outside the boundary of the region's water planning. Data-center water assessment should extend beyond facility withdrawals to electricity-related use and disclosed load data.
