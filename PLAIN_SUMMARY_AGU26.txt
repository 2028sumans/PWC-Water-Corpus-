Data centers are expanding rapidly in regions where water is already stressed, yet facility-level water use is almost never reported publicly. We estimated the water consumed by 243 data-center buildings in Prince William County, Virginia, from public records alone. Fifty-four have been built. Because server halls are fitted out after a building opens, these draw about 7 million gallons per day today, rising toward 45 million at full buildout. Most of that water is never used at the buildings; about 88% is consumed at the power plants generating their electricity, often in another river basin. Where this burden appears depends on how it is counted. One standard method assigns more than 40% to Lake Anna, a reservoir 50 miles away. A second, which asks which plants respond in the short run to new demand, assigns it about one percent. Neither is wrong, but they disagree about which rivers, communities, and regulators are implicated. No operator publishes the electricity data that would narrow these estimates, and the regional water authority's assessment covers only water used at the buildings. Accounting choices thus influence which watershed is treated as responsible, what policies must be made, and which communities must respond.
