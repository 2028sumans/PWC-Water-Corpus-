Data centers are expanding rapidly in regions where water is already stressed, yet almost no facility-level water use is publicly reported. We reconstructed the water footprint of 243 data-center buildings in Prince William County, Virginia, from public records alone. Fifty-four operate today, using an estimated 10 million gallons per day; if everything approved is built, that could reach 50 million.

Most of that water is never used at the buildings. About 87% is consumed at the power plants generating their electricity, often in a different river basin. Where this burden appears turns out to depend on how it is counted. One standard method assigns more than 40% of it to Lake Anna, a reservoir 50 miles away. A second, equally standard method, which asks which power plants actually increase output to serve a new data center, assigns Lake Anna none.

Neither method is wrong, but they implicate different rivers, different communities, and different regulators. Meanwhile no operator publishes the electricity data that would narrow these estimates, and the regional water authority's own assessment covers only water used at the buildings themselves. Which watershed is treated as responsible currently depends on an accounting choice rather than on measurement.
